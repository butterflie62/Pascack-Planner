Make your electives count

Electives & credit types

Some electives can also help meet a graduation requirement. Use this page to see where to start—and what to confirm before you choose a class.

i

Course credit and requirement credit are different.

A course earns credits toward your total. It may also count in a required subject area, but that depends on the specific course. The course catalog does not clearly label every class with every graduation-credit category, so always confirm the exact course with your counselor.

02

Graduation requirement · one year

21st Century Life & Careers

You may hear people call these “practical arts” or career/life-skills classes. The district’s graduation checklist calls the requirement 21st Century Life and Careers.

A course to ask about

21st Century Life Skills

This half-year course covers practical topics such as nutrition, food preparation, clothing, family and community, budgeting, careers, interviews, and communication. Because the graduation requirement is one year, ask whether you need another course to complete it.

03

Graduation requirement · half-year

Financial Literacy

Financial Literacy is its own requirement. The district lists a semester course and a hybrid option.

Business Education Financial Literacy Also listed as a hybrid option. Check with your counselor about the schedule and which version is right for you. View Business Education courses →

Related does not mean interchangeable. A course that discusses budgeting or business does not automatically replace the required Financial Literacy course.

Choosing for interest

What about other electives?

Classes outside these requirement areas can still earn course credits and let you explore interests. Before selecting one, ask whether it counts toward a required category, is an additional elective, or has prerequisites.
